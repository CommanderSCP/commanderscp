# ADR 0028 — Stage-scoped component coupling: trigger-grain holds, CI-declared dependencies, `depends_on` reuse

**Status:** Accepted (owner decisions in chat, 2026-08-05). Detailed design + increments in [docs/proposals/rollout-step-coupling.md](../proposals/rollout-step-coupling.md) v0.2.

**Relates to:** [ADR-0008](0008-observe-enrichment-signals.md) (observe-not-drive — **not amended**, see Decision 1), [ADR-0026](0026-placements-and-derived-stage-names.md) (placements, derived stage names, D8 not-placed-is-declared), [ADR-0006](0006-fail-closed-on-missing-executor-binding-for-purpose.md) (the precedent for declining a trigger), [ADR-0024](0024-decision-and-audit-retention.md) (the write-amplification bug this must not re-open), [coupled-pipelines.md](../proposals/coupled-pipelines.md) (the shipped `provides`/`requires` engine, deliberately untouched), [PROJECT_CHARTER.md](../../PROJECT_CHARTER.md) principles 1 (coordinate, not execute), 2 (graph-native), 3 (API-first parity), 6 (explainability), 7 (Simplicity).

## Context

A service is dozens of microservices, each with its own repo, its own CI, and its own image. They release independently — separate pushes producing separate single-target changes (277 of 281 measured changes target exactly one component, ADR-0026). SCP today has **no way to stop one from deploying ahead of another at a shared place.** The owner's ask: *"if we get to amer-gamma, microservice A won't deploy out until microservice B has done the same"* — without requiring B to reach 100% everywhere, and with the declared dependencies also feeding a real dependency chart instead of guesswork.

A first draft proposed holding A **at a rollout step** (10%, then 50%) until B reached the same step, gated at the wave boundary, declared in the IaC manifest. Grounding against the code refuted the mechanism at four of five load-bearing points; the refutations, with evidence, are proposal §0. Three matter at ADR level:

- **`ExecutorPlugin` is exactly `observe`/`trigger`/`status`/`abort`/`describeCapabilities`** (`packages/plugin-api/src/index.ts:223-231`, and `:196-197` states *"No verb here promotes/pauses/aborts/re-weights a rollout"*). SCP fires one trigger per wave target and thereafter only polls. Once A is triggered, **Argo Rollouts is driving it** — there is no "advance" for SCP to withhold, and the thing that would release an indefinite pause is Argo's `promote`, which ADR-0008 forbids by name.
- **`evaluateWaveGate` produces one verdict for the whole wave** (`reconcile.ts:686-698`; `gates.ts:206-207` keys on `{topologyObjectId, waveIndex}` with no target dimension). In stage mode A and B at `amer-gamma` are in that same wave, so blocking the gate to hold A also holds B — the dependency can never clear. It also fires once per wave, so it cannot re-evaluate.
- **Declaring `depends_on` between two components placed at the same stage breaks plan compilation today**: `plan-compiler.ts:263-280` returns `topology_violates_dependency` → 400 → `auto-cancelled: plan compilation failed`.

## Decision

1. **The coupling holds a TRIGGER, at (component × stage) grain — not a rollout step.** The guarantee is *"A's deploy at stage S is not triggered until every declared dependency of A that applies at S is satisfied at S."* **This does not amend ADR-0008.** SCP adds no verb, never sets a weight, never promotes or aborts a Rollout; it declines to make a call it was always free not to make yet — the authority ADR-0006's binding gate and freezes already exercise. ADR-0008 decision 3 governs *driving* progressive delivery; withholding a trigger is not driving. **A per-step hold is foreclosed, not deferred.**

2. **Enforcement is the per-target seam in the executing loop** (`reconcile.ts:814-830`), beside the trigger-backoff gate — **not** the wave gate (which deadlocks it) and **not** the shipped `waiting`/`provides`/`requires` engine (wrong grain: it parks the whole change on `coordinated → executing`, so A would be held out of dev because B is behind in gamma, and its predicate requires the provider finished everywhere). Two invariants are copied from the backoff gate verbatim: the target is counted as still in flight **before** the `continue`, and the skip happens **before** the advisory trigger-claim lock. **One rule is added that the backoff gate never needed: a held target must not keep an already-FAILED wave alive.** Once every target still in flight is a held one and any target has failed (`failed`, `aborted`, or `no_executor` — the whole class, not the literal), the wave terminalizes as `failed` and the change takes its ordinary failure path; the held target is never triggered on the way there and is left `pending` on the terminal wave. Holding a dependant on a doomed wave buys nothing — its dependency cannot arrive within a wave whose verdict is already decided — and without the rule that wave never reaches `markWaveTerminal` at all: no auto-rollback, no park, no epitaph, and a change wedged in `executing` holding a `BATCH_LIMIT` slot forever, which would be a silent regression on the loud 400 decision 6 removes. The hold writes a Decision with **content-stable** `inputContext` — no live weight, no timestamp — **persisted on change only**, so it cannot re-open the byte-identical-Decision-per-tick write amplification of ADR-0024.

3. **Satisfaction is one universal test plus one optional qualifier.** Universal: the dependency's wave target at that stage has `status = 'succeeded'` — a column every executor produces. **WHICH wave target counts is itself a rule, because plans are compiled at `evaluated -> coordinated`, before anything executes**: a row is an account of a deploy only if it reached a terminal status (`succeeded`/`failed`/`aborted`/`no_executor` — a real attempt, whatever later became of its change) or a change is still actively standing behind it (`executing`, not parked by `markChangeReconcileBlocked`). Anything else — a `pending` row on a change that is `waiting`, `coordinated`, parked, cancelled, or rolled back — is an *intention nobody is acting on*, and being newest it would permanently mask the dependency's genuinely successful earlier deploy at the same place. A genuinely in-flight newer release still supersedes an older success; that is "B is rolling out v2 and A must wait for v2". Optional: `minWeight: N`, satisfied when the dependency's observed canary weight at that stage is `>= N`, which is what lets A proceed at a partial rollout instead of waiting for the stage to finish. **`weight` is the only comparable axis** — `currentStepIndex` is an index into that Rollout's own step list and is meaningless across two components — and it is **structurally absent for blue-green**, so the qualifier is best-effort and phrased *"currently observed at ≥ N"* against an explicit freshness bound off `last_observed_at`.

4. **Three-branch verdict, distinguishable in the Decision.** *Dependency not placed at this stage* → **satisfied** (a declared fact per ADR-0026 D8; failing closed would hold A forever for a correct configuration). *Placed and demonstrably behind* → **hold**. *Signal unreadable* — not ArgoCD, blue/green, extra API call failed, or not yet observed → **proceed with a visible warning**, because unreadable is the default case, not the exception. The unreadable branch applies **only to the `minWeight` qualifier**; an unreadable weight degrades the dependency to the universal `succeeded` test, never to "satisfied".

5. **Dependencies are declared by the microservice's own CI**, as an optional `stageDependencies` collection on the existing typed `ChangeReportRequestSchema` / `CreateChangeRequestSchema`, following the `provides`/`requires` precedent. **Not the IaC manifest** — it is authored in the central stack repo, it is stack-scoped and pruning (so A's repo would have to own and prune A's objects), and relationship `properties` are silently discarded in four places, so per-dependency semantics cannot ride on an edge. **Not a repo-resident file** — SCP fetches no file body from git, and there is no YAML parser in the tree. **Scope is by deployment-target id/URN, never a stage-name glob**: stage names are derived on a UI read path and are `null` for a replicated target, so a name glob would silently match nothing at exactly the federation boundary where the coupling matters.

6. **Declared dependencies materialise as `depends_on` component→component edges, and `plan-compiler.ts`'s same-wave rejection is REPLACED by the new hold** — not deleted. That check is currently the only thing preventing two dependent components deploying in parallel; the per-target hold takes over that duty, and the pair must then compile and serialise. Existing `consumes` edges are left alone (impact analysis reads both). Note that `graph.dependentIds` is a live CEL policy input, so bulk edge writes can flip existing verdicts — the rollout must be observable before it is broad.

   **WHICH SET MOVES, stated exactly, because a replacement that changes the set is a silent regression rather than a refactor.** The removed check keyed on the **edge**: `loadDependsOnEdges` (`plan-service.ts:36-47`) returns every `depends_on` edge with **both endpoints in the change's own target set**, and any such pair landing in one wave was refused — whatever wrote the edge (a seed, an IaC manifest, an operator, or an earlier change's declaration). The hold therefore evaluates the **union** of two sources for each target: (a) the change's own `stageDependencies`, carrying the `minWeight`/`atTargets` qualifiers, and (b) that same in-target-set edge set, with **no** qualifiers — the plain `succeeded` test. It reuses `loadDependsOnEdges` itself, so the two can never drift. **The scope is not one inch wider than what was removed**: an edge with an endpoint *outside* the change's target set ordered nothing before and orders nothing now — which is what keeps a bulk edge import from turning every edge in the org into a release gate. Corollary: `atTargets` narrows the declaration it is written on and cannot subtract the pair's edge; an in-target-set pair a declaration scopes elsewhere still serialises, which is strictly weaker than the 400 it used to get.

   **One clause does NOT move, and is refused at compile time instead: a CYCLE.** With no toposort and no same-wave refusal in stage mode, a mutual pair co-placed at one deployment-target would hold each other forever — a silent permanent deadlock where there used to be a loud 400. `compileStages` refuses a `depends_on` cycle among the change's own targets **that are placed at the same deployment-target**, naming the members and the place, which reconcile turns into the change's epitaph. It is scoped per place because that is precisely where the hold looks: a cycle whose members are never co-placed resolves to `not_placed` on both sides and deadlocks nothing, so refusing it would reject a working configuration.

7. **Deriving dependency charts requires a DECLARED input.** Nothing SCP observes today carries inter-component dependency data: the ArgoCD resource tree models only `{group,version,kind,namespace,name,status,health}`, discovery proposes no dependency edges, and no scan/SBOM code creates graph edges. The declaration channel of Decision 5 *is* the chart's source.

## Consequences

- **Charter principle 1 is satisfied structurally, not by discipline.** The hold lives in the branch that decides whether to *call* `triggerWaveTarget`; it cannot become a drive verb without a new method on a closed interface.
- **The honest guarantee is weaker than "coupled" suggests, and is stated rather than implied.** SCP orders *entries into a place*. Within a place, Argo drives; if a team's canary walks 10→50→100 autonomously, SCP does not interleave A and B at each weight — it only guarantees A does not enter before B is satisfied there. Teams wanting per-weight interleaving must model the weights as separate places, which SCP already coordinates as waves.
- **Additive within /v1**: two optional request fields and an optional response projection; no new change state, no new relationship type, no new table. The oasdiff gate stays green.
- **A live behaviour change**: multi-target changes whose targets include a dependent pair at one stage currently 400 and auto-cancel; after Decision 6 they compile and serialise. That is the intended fix, and it needs its own release-note line. The same line must say what still refuses: a **cycle** among co-placed targets, which is the one shape that could not be handed to the hold.
- **A declaration is an EDGE WRITE, so it carries the edge route's authority.** Decision 6 makes `stageDependencies` a `depends_on` writer, and `graph/relationships-repo.ts`'s `createRelationship` authorizes nothing — so without an explicit check the declaration channel becomes a way to mint the exact edge `POST /relationships` refuses with 403 (its both-endpoint "member_of privilege-escalation guard"), against a component the declarer does not control, permanently (there is no pruning, and the writer treats a soft-delete tombstone as "already materialised"). Every door that accepts a caller-supplied declaration — `POST /changes` and BOTH halves of the persist-then-process ingress — therefore demands `relationship:write` at both endpoints of every edge the declaration would mint, and refuses with 403 rather than silently dropping the entry (a dropped entry is a coupling that vanishes, the failure this feature exists to prevent). `atTargets` is held to `object:read` instead: it mints nothing and only narrows the hold on the declarer's own change, and a write bar there would make the qualifier unusable by the component teams it is for. The ingress checks live at the ROUTE because `webhook-processor.ts` runs as the system actor — the reporting principal exists only at the route — and they are not redundant with the ingress `object:write`, which is a different permission.
- **Explainability**: a held target carries a Decision naming the dependency, the stage, and which of the three branches applied — the backoff gate beside it writes nothing today, so this is a strict improvement in the same loop. **The fail-open is recorded too, not only the hold.** A wave target that is not a `placement` — a legacy-shaped topology, or NO resolvable topology at all, which puts the plan on `compilePlan`'s toposort path and is measurably where a change with placements but no bound pipeline lands — cannot be scoped by a place, so the declaration orders nothing. That branch proceeds (decision 4's warn arm) but now persists its own `warn` Decision (`stage_dependency_unscoped`) naming every unenforced dependency, on the same content-stable / persist-on-change terms as the hold. A declared coupling that did nothing must never be indistinguishable from one that was honoured.
- **Federation is unresolved and must not be silent, so the declaration does not CROSS a promotion.** `change_wave_targets`/`observed_state` are journaled by nothing, reconcile skips foreign-origin changes, and `relationship_upsert` ships only under sync scope `full` — so a confidentiality-scoped outpost would evaluate to "no dependencies" and allow everything. D5 is still open, but the reader shipped, and a promoted change is re-proposed LOCALLY with self origin — so the foreign-origin skip does not exclude it and the outpost really would evaluate the coupling, to `not_placed` → satisfied. **What ships pending D5: `applyPromotionImport` STRIPS `properties.stageDependencies`, exactly as it strips `requires` ([coupled-pipelines.md](../proposals/coupled-pipelines.md) §8 Q2), and records a `stage_dependency` Decision naming what was stripped and that the coupling was enforced upstream at the commander.** That is a deferral, not the ruling: it turns a silent fail-open into a recorded one. `applyPromotionImport` is the seam D5 will change. Detail in proposal §4.1.

## Non-goals

- Holding, pausing, resuming, or re-weighting a running rollout (foreclosed by the verb set and ADR-0008, not deferred).
- Version-aware coupling — "B deployed at S **at the version A expects**" is a real gap and a coherent future ask, deliberately out of scope here.
- A time-series projection of observed rollout state (ADR-0008 deferred it; a freshness bound on the single snapshot is used instead).
- Changing campaigns, which order `depends_on` members but pass no placements and therefore can never be stage-shaped.
- **Per-target scoping of a declaration.** `properties.stageDependencies` hangs off the CHANGE and carries no record of which target an entry was declared for, so every entry is evaluated against every wave target of that change: a change targeting [A, B] where only A's CI declared `dependsOn: C` holds **B** behind C too. Unobservable for the 277-of-281 single-target case decision 5 is written for, where the declaration and the target coincide; wrong exactly for the multi-target change decision 6 re-opened. Not fixed because there is no data to fix it from — a multi-target change arrives with one array and several targets, and `materialiseStageDependencyEdges` already mints an edge from every target, so the breadth is a standing graph fact by then. The shape it would need is one optional `forComponents?: string[]` on `StageDependencySchema` (component ids/URNs, resolved at propose time like `dependsOn`, absent = every target, so existing declarations keep their meaning), filtered beside `atTargets` in the hold and honoured by the edge writer. `atTargets` cannot stand in: that axis is deployment-targets (WHERE), this one is components (WHOSE). The current breadth is pinned by a test so a future narrowing flips a red test rather than changing behaviour silently.
